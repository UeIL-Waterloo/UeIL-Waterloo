Wavefunctions, model limits & sources

Analytic wavefunctions, approximate molecular model. This file evaluates normalized hydrogen-like 1s, 2s and real 2p functions point by point. Their signs, angular nodes, 2s radial node and molecular interference are calculated, not drawn as arbitrary lobes. H₂ uses Z = 1 by default; second-period valence orbitals use illustrative Slater-rule effective charges. They are not Hartree–Fock or correlated molecular orbitals.

In atomic units, with q = Zeffr and u = Zeffx, y or z relative to an atomic centre:

φ₁s = Z³ᐟ² exp(−q)/√π
φ₂s = Z³ᐟ² (2−q) exp(−q/2)/(4√(2π))
φ₂pᵢ = Z³ᐟ² uᵢ exp(−q/2)/(4√(2π))

ψ = (φA + c φB)/√(2 + 2cS), where S = ∫φAφB d³r and c = ±1. The x axis joins the nuclei. Both atomic p axes use the same global orientation. Thus σ₂pₓ bonding uses subtraction (c = −1); π₂p bonding uses addition. The displayed overlap is signed for this convention. Numerical cylindrical Gauss–Legendre quadrature evaluates S; the main viewer extracts equal-amplitude surfaces from a three-dimensional grid of the analytic wavefunction using marching tetrahedra. Wavefunction mode extracts ψ = ±k using a fraction of peak |ψ|. Density mode explicitly squares the complete normalized LCAO function, including its interference term, and extracts ρ = |ψ|² at a fraction of peak ρ. Each mode remembers its own threshold. By default the reference peak is held fixed while separation or effective Z changes (in scaled coordinates); use “Set reference at current separation” to update it, or uncheck the lock to rescale continuously. Peak estimates use analytic AO maxima or a mesh-independent search of the MO amplitude. AO meshes move rigidly with their centres, and MO grid spacing stays fixed as separation changes. These choices avoid grid-related size jumps. Physical nodal changes and lobe merging can still occur; MO lobe dimensions need not be monotonic with separation. For example, 8% of peak amplitude corresponds to 0.64% of peak density; 8% of peak density corresponds to about 28.3% of peak amplitude. Surfaces at these matched thresholds coincide up to mesh discretization, as they must. Equal percentage settings in the two modes generally yield different surfaces. Neither threshold is an enclosed probability. The quoted physical levels include the Z³ᐟ² amplitude or Z³ density scaling. The positive and negative phase regions are meshed separately to preserve nodes even though density itself is nonnegative. Atomic-orbital mode shows the two individual AO surfaces. Shading indicates surface orientation; colours encode wavefunction sign (or uniform colour on the recalculated density surface). Orbital surfaces are opaque. The Cutaway control removes the z > 0 half of both orbitals and node guides to reveal interiors; rotate the view to inspect the opening. Yellow 2s-derived nodal surfaces have their own visibility control: atomic nodes are spheres at r = 2a₀/Zeff, while molecular surfaces follow numerically solved zero contours of the full 2s LCAO function, revolved about the bond axis. The antibonding midplane is excluded from the yellow mesh and shown separately in violet. Node surfaces are independent of the nonzero amplitude/density surface threshold. The mesh is a numerical approximation to the analytic isosurface.

The energy diagram uses the standard introductory valence MO ordering: π₂p below σ₂p for Li₂–N₂; σ₂p below π₂p for O₂–F₂. This reversal is associated with changing s–p mixing. Actual mixed σ orbitals require more than one 2s/2p pair: that mixing is represented in the diagram ordering only, not in the pairwise wavefunction shapes. Energies are schematic, are not computed from the displayed wavefunctions, and do not change with R. Neutral ground-state filling is the starting configuration. Adding or removing electrons updates bond order and charge without automatically relaxing the configuration or recalculating energies and orbital shapes. Arbitrary edited occupations can describe excited configurations; they are not predictions of stable ions or a dissociation calculation. The two occupied 1s core MOs of second-period molecules cancel in bond order and are omitted.

“Bring together” ends at the experimental neutral ground-state bond length, independent of the selected orbital, electron count and effective Z. The initial view is separated; manual dragging and the slider allow other separations. Distances are displayed in Å and converted to Bohr radii using 1 a₀ = 0.529177210903 Å. The target is a measured reference, not an optimization of this LCAO model or a prediction for an ion or excited state. Targets (Å): H₂ 0.741; Li₂ 2.673; Be₂ 2.454; B₂ 1.590; C₂ 1.243; N₂ 1.098; O₂ 1.208; F₂ 1.412. Values are rounded to 0.001 Å from NIST CCCBDB experimental diatomic bond lengths, except Be₂, whose equilibrium distance is taken from the spectroscopic potential of Merritt, Bondybey & Heaven (2009), Table 1 (rₑ = 2.453603 Å); the older NIST Be₂ entry is an r₀ value. Bond order zero for Be₂ means no bond in this elementary model; the real weak Be₂ bond requires a more complete treatment. The spatial scale is fixed while dragging; “Fit view” reframes it. Fluorescent-green guides mark angular nodal planes and the curved p-derived zero surfaces of σ₂pₓ and σ*₂pₓ. In atomic-orbital mode the two pₓ nodes are planes through their own nuclei. In molecular mode the axially symmetric equation φA ± φB = 0 is solved numerically and revolved about x; the shown patches extend to a scaled radial distance of 9. For σ*₂pₓ the additional sheets merge into the midplane when R·Zeff ≤ 4 in this pairwise hydrogen-like basis; violet guides mark antibonding internuclear nodes. Guide planes are translucent and depth-tested against the balloons; the evaluated density also contains other nodes, including 2s-derived nodal surfaces. All functions use a common adjustable Zeff per molecule. No external scripts or internet connection are required.

References: OpenStax, Molecular Orbital Theory; University of Illinois, Molecular Orbital Theory. Wavefunctions are the standard analytic hydrogenic solutions.
